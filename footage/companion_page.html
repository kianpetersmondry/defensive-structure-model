3-D Animation Model

The same defensive-structure model that drives the match animations, run on the actual TV pictures instead of a 2-D pitch. Players are found in the broadcast, placed on the real pitch, and the read is drawn back onto them: team markers, the inter-line band, the ball-side marking zone, the pressure ring and the danger heatmap.

What's drawn

Player markers under every tracked player, in team colour. Shows what the model can actually see.
Inter-line band, deepest to most advanced defender. Drawn only when all 10 outfield defenders are in shot.
TIGHT marking zone: the 5 defenders nearest the ball span 23 m or less.
LOOSE marking zone: the same group is spread wider than 23 m.
Pressure ring round the ball carrier, solid when the defence is ENGAGED (smoothed pressure ≥ 0.67).
Dashed when it's PASSIVE. Colour runs from grey to amber to red as pressure rises.
Danger heatmap, tinted to the team on the ball. Shown once possession has been stable for 1.5 s.
NO READBadges when there's nothing to read: close-ups and other camera angles, replays, or not all 10 defenders in shot.

The 10-player rule

A defensive phase (High Press, Mid Block, Low Block, Transition), the inter-line band and the depth / team-length numbers are only shown when all 10 outfield players of the defending team are in shot, and the pitch behind and in front of them is visible too. With part of the team off-screen the model could call a High Press off the players it happens to see, so it doesn't guess. A broadcast camera rarely frames a whole team, which is why the phase label is missing most of the time: that's the camera's limit made visible, not the model failing. A defender hidden behind another player for under a third of a second doesn't break the read, as long as all 10 are in shot either side of it. Pressure, ENGAGED/PASSIVE, TIGHT/LOOSE and the heatmap only need the players around the ball, so they keep running.

How it works

  1. Find the players and the ball in every third frame of the broadcast with an object detector.
  2. Work out where the camera is looking. The main camera sits in one place and only pans, tilts and zooms, so its position is solved once from the pitch lines, then its angle and zoom are fitted frame by frame to the white markings.
  3. Put everyone on the pitch. Each player's feet are mapped to real pitch coordinates, sorted into France, Morocco, keepers and referee by kit colour, and followed from frame to frame. Identities restart after every camera cut.
  4. Run the same model as the animations: the same phase thresholds, pressure formula, ENGAGED/PASSIVE and TIGHT/LOOSE cut-offs, and danger heatmap.
  5. Draw it back onto the video, painted only on the grass so players and pitch lines stay on top.

What it can't do yet

  • The broadcast shows 10-15 of the 22 players at a time, so the full shape was readable for only about 11 seconds of these five minutes of play.
  • The camera is tracked on about three-quarters of live play; close-ups and the pitch-level and reverse-angle cameras are left unread.
  • Replays are recognised and skipped, not analysed.
  • Kit colours, the camera position and the attacking direction are set per match, so a new stadium or club broadcast needs its own set-up.
