Space opened behind the line, by phase

For every half-second in the animation's 5-minute window, how much of the pitch behind the defending team's back line was both reachable by a single pass from the ball's current spot and dangerous (close to goal) — the DAS (Dangerous Accessible Space) model, evaluated only on the strip between the last defender and their own byline. Log scale: most moments carry very little live danger, and the interesting story is in the spikes.

Why High Press looks "quiet" here isn't a bug, and why phases aren't ranked against each other. This metric only counts danger the ball could reach with one realistic pass right now. While a team is still pressing, the ball is usually still up near the press itself, a long way from the space opening up behind it — the danger shows up once the block breaks down, which is why spikes cluster in Mid Block and Defensive Transition. But there's no fair single number for "which phase is worst": a deeper block's space-in-behind is, by definition, closer to goal, so it will always price out as more "dangerous" per the value model than a high line's much larger but further-out gap — that's a property of geography, not of which phase is riskier to play. Read each facet for its own spikes and timing, not as a ranking against the others.
